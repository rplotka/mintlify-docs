Learning In Stride provides two grading surfaces: **Speed Grader** for reviewing individual submissions with inline feedback, and the **Grading Command Center** for managing grade distribution and team workload at scale.

## Speed Grader

Speed Grader opens when you click **Grade** on any assignment in the assignment manager. It displays one submission at a time in a three-panel layout:

| Panel | Content |
|---|---|
| Left | Submission preview (text, file, or URL) |
| Center | Rubric scoring or simple score input |
| Right | AI Chat, Grade History, or Student context |

### Navigating submissions

Use the arrow buttons in the header to move between submissions, or use keyboard shortcuts:

| Action | Shortcut |
|---|---|
| Previous submission | `←` or `k` |
| Next submission | `→` or `j` |
| Save grade | `⌘S` |
| Save and advance | `⌘Enter` |

The progress indicator in the header shows how many submissions you have graded out of the total (e.g., `12 / 34`).

### Rubric-based grading

When the assignment has a rubric, the center panel shows each rubric category and its criteria. Click the score for each criterion to fill in your assessment. The total calculates automatically as you score.

Use the **feedback** field below the rubric to write a comment that students will see alongside their score.

### Point-based grading

If the assignment has no rubric, the center panel shows a single numeric score field. Enter a value up to the assignment's maximum points, add optional feedback, and save.

### The side panel

Toggle the side panel with the icon in the header (or the floating button on mobile). Three tabs are available:

- **AI Chat** — ask the AI questions about the current submission. Use **Insert Feedback** on any AI response to append it directly to the feedback field. Requires an AI add-on.
- **History** — a timeline of all previous grades and feedback for this submission, including AI-suggested scores and human overrides.
- **Student** — aggregated context for the student: overall grade, participation score, prior submissions, and any at-risk flags.

### Late submissions

Submissions past the due date are labeled **Late** in the student info bar. The late penalty defined for the assignment applies automatically when you save the grade. The grade history records the raw score and the post-penalty score separately.

### Saving grades

Click **Save** to record the grade without advancing, or **Save & Next** to record and move to the next submission. Grades saved in Speed Grader are immediately visible to students unless the assignment is set to hide grades until a release date.

## Grading Command Center

Access the Grading Command Center from **Hub → Grading** (available to Instructors, TAs, and Graders).

**Instructors** see a course-wide overview: grading progress per assignment, distribution of work among TAs and Graders, and overall completion percentages.

**TAs and Graders** see their personal assignment queue, progress statistics, and any grading threads assigned to them.

Use the Grading Command Center to:
- Identify which assignments still have ungraded submissions.
- Distribute grading work to TAs and Graders.
- Monitor team progress without opening individual assignments.

## Student Grades roster

Go to **Admin → Courses → [course] → Grades** to see the full grade roster for a course. The roster shows:

- Each student's overall grade percentage and letter-grade performance level (A, B, C, D, F).
- A mini trend line showing weekly assignment score trajectory.
- Participation score based on Discord activity and attendance.
- Grade count (number of graded items contributing to the average).

**Filters and sorting** — search by name, RCS ID, or RIN. Filter by performance level. Sort by grade, name, or participation score.

**Student detail panel** — click any student to open a side panel with their full grade breakdown across assignments, presentations, and participation.

## Grade weights and categories

Final grade weights are managed through the **Final Grading** section of your course settings. Navigate to **Admin → Courses → [course] → Grading → Grade Weights**. From there you can:

- Define grade categories (e.g., Assignments, Presentations, Participation).
- Set the percentage weight for each category.
- Review the calculated final grade for each student.

The weights you configure apply to all final grade calculations for the course semester.

## Exporting grades

Go to **Admin → Export** to download grade data. Available formats include CSV and Excel. You can export:

- All grades for a single assignment.
- Full gradebook for a course (all categories, all students).
- Aggregated final grades ready for SIS submission.

<Tip>
  Run an export before the grade submission deadline as a backup. The export reflects grades at the moment of download.
</Tip>

## Handling grade inquiries

When a student questions a grade, open Speed Grader for their submission and review the **History** tab. The timeline shows each scoring event — including who graded, when, and whether an AI suggestion was accepted or overridden — giving you a complete audit trail to share with the student.

To update a grade after the initial save, simply re-open Speed Grader and enter a new score. The history tab records the change.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can TAs grade without seeing other students' scores?">
    Yes. TAs see only the submissions in their queue and do not have access to the full grade roster unless you grant them an Instructor role.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How does the overall grade calculate?">
    The overall grade shown in the Student Grades roster is a weighted average of all graded items, based on the grade weights you configured. Items with no grade yet are excluded from the running average.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens if I change a rubric after grading starts?">
    Changing rubric criteria after submissions have been graded may cause the calculated total to shift. Review existing grades in the Speed Grader history before modifying a live rubric.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Are grades visible to students immediately?">
    Yes, by default. If you want to batch-release grades (e.g., after all submissions are graded), set a grade release date on the assignment before any grades are saved.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
